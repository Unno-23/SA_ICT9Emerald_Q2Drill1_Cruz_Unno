Beginner's Guide to World Rally Championship



Automobile competition over a specified public route with a driver and navigator attempting to keep to a predetermined schedule between checkpoints. The course is generally unknown to contestants until the start of the rally, read more.

Basic Rules

  • The navigator provides directions and timing information.
  • Each car requires a two-person crew: a driver and a co-driver.
  • The crew with the lowest cumulative time across all special stages wins the rally.

Requirements

  • Any individual holding a valid and legal public road driving permit can technically start rallying.
  • To compete in the WRC, drivers and co-drivers must obtain an official FIA international competition license.
  • A co-driver is mandatory for all events to handle navigation, timekeeping, and reading pace notes.

Practice

  • Practice car physics, weight transfer, and loose-surface sliding using games.
  • Turn off on-screen track symbols and practice listening to pace notes early.
  • Train your arms to feel what the front tires are doing on gravel rather than relying on sight.
